# Waiting Well

## A Short Guide to Doing Nothing (Productively)

---

You've received or are about to receive money. You're not sure what to do with it. You're wondering if it's okay to wait.

**Yes. It's okay to wait.**

This is the guide for that.

---

## The Question You're Asking

> "Is it okay to wait because I don't know?"

**Answer:** Yes. Not knowing is not a failure. It's a starting point.

Most money advice assumes you should act. This guide assumes the opposite: that **not acting yet** is often the smartest thing you can do—as long as you're waiting well, not just avoiding.

---

## Two Kinds of Waiting

| Waiting Well | Waiting Badly |
|--------------|---------------|
| "I'm giving this time to settle." | "I'm hoping it goes away." |
| "I don't need to decide yet." | "I'm scared to look at it." |
| "I'm learning while I wait." | "I'm avoiding so I don't have to think." |
| "I'm noticing my feelings without acting on them." | "I'm pretending I don't have feelings." |
| Active pause | Paralysis |

**Waiting Well = intentional not-deciding.**

**Waiting Badly = unintentional not-deciding.**

If you're reading this, you're probably doing the first one.

---

## Why Waiting Is Smart

### 1. You Can't Undo Most Big Decisions

Money moves easily in one direction: out. Getting it back, or undoing a commitment, is much harder.

| Easy to Do | Hard to Undo |
|------------|--------------|
| Buy something | Sell it for what you paid |
| Lend to a friend | Get it back without awkwardness |
| Invest in something | Exit at the right time |
| Tell people | Un-tell them |

Waiting keeps your options open. That has real value.

### 2. Your First Instinct Might Not Be Your Best Instinct

When you first receive money, you're often in a heightened state—excited, anxious, pressured. Decisions made in that state often look different 6 months later.

**Give yourself time to move from reaction to reflection.**

### 3. Clarity Comes From Living, Not Just Thinking

Sometimes you don't know what you want because you haven't encountered the situation yet. Life experience will teach you things that sitting and planning won't.

**Waiting lets you gather information from actually living.**

### 4. There's No Prize for Speed

No one is scoring you on how quickly you optimised your money. The financial advice industry creates urgency because urgency sells products.

**Your actual situation has no deadline.**

- [ ] Do nothing with the money for now
- [ ] Not have a plan

- [ ] Feel weird about this

Signed: *Reality*

## What to Actually Do While Waiting

### The Minimum (5 minutes total)

1. **Put the money somewhere safe and boring**
   - High-interest savings account (no lock-in period)
   - The interest rate doesn't matter much right now
   - The point is: it's safe, it's accessible, it's not going anywhere

2. **That's it**

Seriously. If you do only this, you're fine.

### The Optional Extras (If You Feel Like It)

**Super check (15 minutes):**
- Log into your super account(s)
- Are fees under 1%? (Probably fine if yes)
- If you have multiple super accounts from old jobs, consider consolidating to reduce fees

**Beneficiary nomination (5 minutes):**
- While you're in your super, check who gets it if something happens to you
- Probably want it to be who you think it should be

**Brief money hygiene:**
- Is your bank account earning any interest at all? (If no, maybe move to one that does)
- Do you have more than $250k? If so, consider splitting across institutions for government guarantee coverage

**None of this is urgent.** It's just stuff that's easy to do and doesn't require any real decisions.

---

## What NOT to Do While Waiting

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Making major purchases to "get it over with" | Impulsive decisions often regretted |
| Lending large amounts to friends/family | Hard to undo; relationship-altering |
| Giving control to someone else to manage | You lose visibility and learning |
| Investing in things you don't understand | Being confused is not a good starting point |
| Telling everyone | Creates pressure; invites opinions you didn't ask for |
| Comparing yourself to others who seem certain | Their certainty is often performance |
| Beating yourself up for not knowing | Not knowing is normal |

## How Long Is Too Long?

There's no specific answer. But here are some signals:

**Waiting is probably still fine if:**
- You're thinking about it occasionally, not obsessively
- You're slowly getting clearer, even if still uncertain
- The money is safe and not losing significant value
- You're not avoiding it out of fear

**Waiting might have become avoidance if:**
- You feel genuine dread when you think about it
- It's been a long time and you're no clearer than day one
- You're actively not looking at it
- The uncertainty is causing real distress

**If it's become avoidance:** That's okay. It's information. Might be time to talk to someone—a fee-only financial adviser for the practical stuff, or a therapist/counsellor if it's the emotional stuff that's stuck.

## The "What If They Ask?" Script

If family or others ask what you're doing with the money:

**Simple version:**
> "I'm taking my time with it. No rush."

**If they push:**
> "I'm not ready to make any big decisions yet. I'll figure it out when I'm ready."

**If they offer advice:**
> "Thanks. I'll think about it."

(You don't have to think about it.)

**If they express concern:**
> "I appreciate that. I'm okay."

You don't owe anyone a plan. You don't owe anyone updates. "I'm taking my time" is a complete answer.

---

## When You Might Be Ready to Decide Something

You'll probably start to feel ready when:

- You've been thinking about a specific thing for weeks (not just days)
- The thought feels more exciting than anxious
- You could explain why, even if imperfectly
- It doesn't feel impulsive—it feels considered
- You've sat with the idea long enough that you trust it

**There's no timer. There's no test. Ready when you're ready.**

---

## The One Reframe

You might be thinking: *"I should know what to do. Other people know what to do. What's wrong with me?"*

Try this instead: *"I'm taking this seriously. That's why I'm not rushing."*

The people who confidently know exactly what to do are often:
- Following someone else's script without questioning it
- More reckless than wise
- Performing certainty they don't actually feel
- Just different from you (not better)

**Your uncertainty is not a bug. It's a feature.**

## The Shortest Version

1. Put money in a high-interest savings account
2. Don't do anything else
3. Wait until you're ready
4. "Ready" means: you've thought about something for weeks, it feels right, and you can explain why
5. Until then, you're fine
