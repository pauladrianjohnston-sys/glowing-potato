# Your Money, Your Pace

## A Private Reflection Workbook

*For young adults receiving family wealth*

---

## Before You Start

**This is yours.** No one will ask about it. There's no grade, no completion requirement, no "right" answers.

**You can:**
- Do it all at once or spread it over weeks
- Skip sections that don't resonate
- Come back to it later (or never)
- Write nothing down and just think
- Disagree with the framing entirely

**This workbook assumes:**
- You didn't ask for this money (or maybe you did, but it still feels complicated)
- You might not know what to do, and that's fine
- Waiting is a valid strategy
- Your feelings about this are probably mixed

**What this is not:**
- Financial advice
- A plan you need to follow
- Something to perform for anyone
- Homework

**Yes.** Waiting is not avoiding. Waiting is a choice.

### 4.3 "What Do I Do While I'm Waiting?"

See Part 5 for a specific holding-pattern checklist. But briefly:

**Things that are almost always fine to do while deciding nothing:**
- [ ] Park the money in a high-interest savings account
- [ ] Check your super fund is reasonable
- [ ] Not tell people (privacy is fine)
- [ ] Read, watch, or listen to things about money (see Resources)
- [ ] Notice your feelings without acting on them
- [ ] Do nothing at all

**Things that can usually wait:**
- [ ] Major purchases
- [ ] Investing (unless you're clear you want to)
- [ ] Telling friends
- [ ] Deciding your whole life plan
- [ ] Having everything figured out

### 4.4 "What If I Make a Mistake?"

You probably will. Most people do. It's how learning works.

**The question isn't "how do I avoid mistakes?"**

The question is: **"What kind of mistakes can I recover from, and what kind would actually harm me?"**

| Recoverable | Harder to Recover |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Spending some on something that doesn't bring joy | Spending all of it impulsively |
| Investing in something that loses value | Giving control to someone untrustworthy |
| Being slow to act | Locking yourself into something irreversible |
| Not optimising returns | Taking on debt you can't escape |

**The goal isn't perfection. It's staying in a position where you can keep learning.**

## Part 5: The Holding-Pattern Checklist

*What to do when you're not ready to decide.*

### 5.1 The "Good Enough For Now" Setup

None of this is permanent. It's just a stable place to wait.

**Money location:**
- [ ] Park funds in a high-interest savings account (no lock-in)
- [ ] Don't do anything that can't be undone for 6+ months

**Super check:**
- [ ] Look at your superannuation fund once
- [ ] Are the fees under 1%? (If yes, probably fine for now)
- [ ] Is it in a reasonable option for your age? (Usually "balanced" or "growth")
- [ ] If you have multiple super accounts from different jobs, consider consolidating (but no rush)

**Protection basics:**
- [ ] Do you have a will? (Not urgent at 20, but worth knowing)
- [ ] Is your super beneficiary nominated? (Takes 5 minutes online)

**That's it.** Everything else can wait until you're ready.

### 5.2 What You Don't Need to Do

- [ ] Invest immediately (or ever, if you don't want to)
- [ ] Buy property
- [ ] Have a 10-year financial plan
- [ ] Tell anyone
- [ ] Maximise returns
- [ ] Feel grateful in a specific way
- [ ] Know what you want
- [ ] Do what your cousins are doing
- [ ] Take advice from people who seem confident

### 5.3 Things to Avoid (Not Rules, Just Cautions)

**Generally best to avoid while still deciding:**

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Lending significant amounts to friends | Very hard to undo; changes relationships |
| Co-signing loans for others | Legal exposure you can't easily escape |
| Giving control to someone else to manage | You lose visibility and learning |
| Major irreversible purchases (unless you're certain) | Locks up capital; often regretted |
| Telling everyone | Creates pressure; invites opinions |
| Crypto or high-risk speculation (with money you'd miss) | The point of a holding pattern is stability |

**This isn't about being boring.** It's about not closing doors while you figure out which ones you want to walk through.

### 5.4 Signals You Might Be Ready to Decide Something

- You notice you've been thinking about a specific thing for weeks (not just days)
- The thought of doing it feels more exciting than anxious
- You could explain why you want to do it (even if the explanation isn't perfect)
- You've sat with the idea long enough that it doesn't feel impulsive

**There's no timer.** Ready when you're ready.

## Closing Notes

### What You've Done

You've spent time thinking about your own situation. That's more than most people do.

### What You Haven't Done

Made any commitments. Decided anything. Promised anyone anything.

### What Happens Next

Whatever you want. This workbook has no sequel. There's no level 2.

If you want resources: there's a curated list of books, podcasts, videos, and films in the Resources document—all non-preachy, all designed to help you think, not tell you what to do.

If you want to talk to someone: that's fine. A fee-only financial adviser (not commission) for mechanics, or a financial therapist for the psychological stuff. See the Resources document for how to vet them.

If you want to do nothing: also fine.

### A Final Note

The fact that you're uncertain is not a problem to solve. It's a sign that you're taking this seriously.

Certainty about money usually comes from either not thinking about it, or from outsourcing your thinking to someone else's rules.

Your uncertainty is the beginning of your own judgement. That's worth more than someone else's confident answer.
